Automotive research: agentic AI adoption outpaces mature governance

2026-10-12 | Capgemini Research Institute; original publication date from structured metadata

Capgemini Research Institute surveyed 800 executives at automotive manufacturers, suppliers and technology providers across 12 countries, supplemented by 24 engineering-leader interviews. Among automotive OEMs and suppliers working towards AI adoption, 35% report using agentic AI in engineering and 33% are beginning pilots. Only 5% of organizations describe their AI governance as fully mature; the adoption percentages should not be read as covering every automotive firm.

The research associates stronger results with higher-quality engineering data, clearer accountability and governance. It reports leading organizations achieving two to three times greater gains in development speed, productivity and time-to-market, while highlighting disconnected engineering systems and difficulty turning vehicle telemetry into engineering action. These are survey findings, not independently verified delivery benchmarks.

Source: Capgemini research

Interpretation and possible follow-up, not reported facts.

Why it matters: Capgemini frames data foundations and governance as barriers to scaling agentic AI.

Follow-up: Use the findings as a discussion starter for joint automotive accounts.

US suspends Capgemini from the PERM green-card program

2026-10-08 | CNBC

The US Department of Labor announced on October 8 that it would suspend Capgemini, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata, Wipro and HCL from its Permanent Labor Certification program. CNBC reports that the department would stop accepting or processing new applications. Microsoft and Adobe were also suspended, with the Labor Secretary citing active federal investigations for those two companies.

The announcement came during a broader administration crackdown on foreign-worker hiring. Microsoft disputed claims that it was replacing American employees, saying 80% of its roughly 6,000 H-1B applications in the previous fiscal year concerned existing staff. Those figures describe Microsoft, not Capgemini; the cited report does not quantify Capgemini's affected employees or confirm disruption to its client delivery.

Source: CNBC

Interpretation and possible follow-up, not reported facts.

Why it matters: Pressure on Capgemini's US staffing and onshore delivery warrants checking specific engagements rather than assuming disruption.

2026-10-10 | Capgemini announcement; underlying Gartner report dated September 9

HFS names Capgemini a Horizon 3 GCC Orchestrator

2026-10-08 | Capgemini

The study assessed 24 providers on helping enterprises turn delivery centres into AI-enabled, outcome-driven operations. The announcement also describes agentic AI, digital twins and MLOps as transformation capabilities. It names cost and deployment benefits but supplies no numerical results for individual customers; this is analyst recognition, not a new client contract.

Capgemini joins Anthropic's first Frontier Academy cohorts

2026-10-02 | Anthropic

Anthropic launched Claude Frontier Academy with a $100 million commitment and a target of training 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027. Capgemini is named among the first participating organizations, alongside Accenture, Bain, Deloitte and others. The announcement includes a Capgemini executive's endorsement of hands-on work with Anthropic engineers to strengthen client delivery.

Participants begin with in-person training and a practical assessment, followed by a 12-week residency delivering a real Claude use case at their own organization. A second assessment leads to the final credential, with the first expected in early 2027. The 10,000 target covers the entire program; Anthropic does not disclose Capgemini's cohort size or customer outcomes.

Investigation examines HMRC's long-running Capgemini spending

2026-09-28 | The Register

The Register and procurement-data provider Otnox analysed HMRC's published transactions above GBP 25,000 and reported at least GBP 4.2 billion paid to Capgemini between 2014 and July 2026. The total spans 15,726 transactions, with sixteen months of data missing. This is new reporting on historical spending, not a GBP 4.2 billion contract awarded during this week.

The article traces HMRC's effort to replace the Aspire outsourcing arrangement while continuing to procure Capgemini services for critical systems and modernization. HMRC told the publication its awards complied with procurement law and government policy, and said its supplier base had become more diverse. The analysis highlights incumbent relationships but does not establish procurement wrongdoing.

Source: The Register

Why it matters: Useful account context for public-sector modernization, with historical spending kept distinct from new pipeline.

Payments research puts $230 billion of bank revenue at risk

2026-09-24 | Capgemini Research Institute

Capgemini's World Payments Report 2027 projects that stablecoins, tokenized deposits and central bank digital currencies could represent approximately 4% of global payments volume by 2030, putting $230 billion of bank payments revenue at risk. The study surveyed 1,110 large corporates and 300 banking executives across nine markets. Nearly three-quarters of corporate respondents describe cross-border payments as slow, costly and unpredictable.

Banks remain the preferred provider at equivalent cost and quality, but nearly 60% of corporates would consider non-bank stablecoin services if banks lag. Only 21% of surveyed banks are actively scaling at least one of these instruments. The revenue exposure is a research projection, not a reported loss or a Capgemini sales forecast.

Source: Capgemini

Team take Suggested

Interpretation and possible follow-up, not reported facts.

Why it matters: Payment modernization creates discussions about real-time visibility, governed data and compliance across banking systems.

Sweden selects Capgemini to integrate its emergency network

2026-09-23 | The Critical Communications Review

The Critical Communications Review reported that Sweden's Civil Defense Agency signed an agreement with Capgemini to act as system integrator for SWEN, the Swedish Emergency Network. Its role includes coordinating technical solutions, quality assurance, technical project management and coordination between suppliers. The agency said integration work was under way and the program remained on schedule.

SWEN is intended to replace the TETRA-based Rakel network by 2030, using technology partly based on 3GPP-standardized 5G. Existing suppliers include Ericsson for the core network, Telia for initial pilot radio access and Airbus for migration interoperability. Further procurements remained outstanding, including the MCX platform expected around mid-2027. The article does not disclose Capgemini's contract value.

Source: The Critical Communications Review

Team take Suggested

Interpretation and possible follow-up, not reported facts.

Why it matters: A concrete public-sector integration engagement involving secure interoperability and multiple technology suppliers.
